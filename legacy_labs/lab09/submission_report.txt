LAB 9.1 - WRITE CONCERN LATENCY

Method: sequentially insert 1,000 small documents for each configuration in a
three-member MongoDB 7 replica set. Average latency is total insert-call time
divided by 1,000. The table shows one measured local Docker run; results vary
with storage, scheduling, and host load.

Configuration                    Total (s)   Average (ms/write)   P50 (ms)   P99 (ms)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
w:1, j:false                       1.054           1.053           0.625      7.282
w:1, j:true                       11.261          11.258           2.030     32.336
w:majority, j:false               12.177          12.173           6.774     68.531
w:majority, j:true                 9.382           9.378           4.658     45.005
w:3 (all members), j:false         7.741           7.738           2.937     43.571
w:3 (all members), j:true         14.820          14.813           5.569     72.678

MongoDB accepts numeric w values, "majority", or configured tag modes; it does
not accept a literal w:"all". For this three-member replica set, w:3 waits for
all three data-bearing members. w:1 was fastest in this run because it waits
only for the primary. Majority and w:3 took longer overall because the primary
waits for replication acknowledgments. j:true requests journal persistence.
The stronger replication concerns took longer overall, consistent with waiting
for member acknowledgments. w:majority with j:true happened to be faster than
with j:false, while w:1 with j:true had a high average despite a much lower
median. This non-monotonic result illustrates the effect of outliers and
scheduling in a single run; rerun the benchmark and compare medians before
drawing conclusions about small differences.

LAB 9.2 - NETWORK PARTITION

The lab disconnected mongo2 for 30 seconds. The primary and mongo3 remained
connected and retained a voting majority. Both probe writes succeeded:
  w:1        acknowledged in 17.33 ms
  w:majority acknowledged in 74.31 ms

During the partition, rs.status() on mongo1 showed mongo1 PRIMARY, mongo2
"(not reachable/healthy)" with health 0, and mongo3 SECONDARY with health 1.
After reconnection, mongo2 returned as SECONDARY. This shows that one member
can be isolated without losing the majority in a three-member set. Losing both
secondaries would remove the primary's majority; after the election timeout
the primary steps down and majority writes cannot be acknowledged.

Reproduce the demonstrations:
  ./labctl.sh up
  ./labctl.sh benchmark
  ./labctl.sh partition
